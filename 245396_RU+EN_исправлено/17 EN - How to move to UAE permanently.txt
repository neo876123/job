<p>The UAE offers numerous residence visa options, valid for up to 10 years. Foreigners can legalise their status through employment, proving monthly income that meets specific requirements, enrolling in an accredited university, family reunification, or investments starting from AED 500,000 ($136,000). Property buyers investing AED 2 million ($544,500) can obtain a 10-year Golden Visa with renewal options. In contrast, an investment of AED 750,000 ($204,000) in <a href="[:ALIAS_URL:]/property/uae/dubai/">property in Dubai</a>, one of the best cities for living in the UAE, qualifies for a 2-year residency.</p>
<p>Foreigners who have legally resided in the Emirates for 30 years may apply for citizenship. The naturalisation period for those of Arab origin is reduced to 3-7 years. Since 2021, citizenship can be offered by nomination to investors who own property in the UAE, as well as to doctors, scientists, inventors and cultural figures. There is no fixed investment amount, and you cannot apply yourself: candidates are put forward by the emirates' authorities.</p>

<h3>Property purchase</h3>
<p>A golden visa can be issued based on investments in real estate located in any of the emirates. Buyers of apartments and <a href="[:ALIAS_URL:]/property/uae/dubai/villas/">villas in Dubai</a> can participate in a separate programme. An investment of AED 750,000 ($204,000) enables them to obtain a 2-year residence permit, with the option to extend their status. The investor can relocate to the UAE with their family, including a spouse, unmarried daughters of any age, and sons under the age of 25.</p>
<p>A case from Middle East's practice: in 2025, a one-bedroom apartment in an off-plan complex in Dubai cost the buyer about AED&nbsp;1.03&nbsp;million (around $281,500), which is above the threshold for the 2-year residence permit. The developer split the payment as follows: 10% on signing, another 10% three months later and the rest on a schedule running to the end of 2027. Whether an off-plan unit counts for the visa before completion should be checked before buying, with the developer and the Dubai Land Department.</p>
<p>"Buyers often expect a Golden Visa with an apartment worth AED&nbsp;1–1.5&nbsp;million, but it requires a valuation of at least AED&nbsp;2&nbsp;million. If the budget is smaller, it is more honest to talk about the 2-year residence permit for AED&nbsp;750,000 straight away: it can also be renewed, but the status has to be confirmed more often," says a residential property broker in Dubai.</p>
<h3>Green Visa</h3>
<p>The Green Visa does not require sponsorship from citizens or residents. It is valid for 5 years. It can be obtained through an investment of AED 1 million ($272,500) or more in creating a new business or supporting an existing one in the country. This residency visa in the UAE can be issued to the investor's spouse, unmarried daughters of any age, and sons under the age of 25. The status can be extended an unlimited number of times.</p>
<h3>Employment</h3>
<p>Foreign employees of UAE companies can obtain a work residence permit. The employer submits the application to the Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation or the free economic zone, depending on the type of legal entity. Once approved, the foreigner will be issued an entry permit.</p>
<p>Upon arrival in the country, the employee receives an arrival stamp. Information about them is sent to the Immigration Service. Next, they must undergo a medical examination and submit biometric data. If the expat meets all requirements, they will be issued a residence permit within a few weeks.</p>
<p>Specialists who meet the following conditions can access a Green Visa:</p>
<ul>
<li>having a valid employment contract with a local company;</li>
<li>a level of professional qualification that meets the requirements of the Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation;</li>
<li>a bachelor's degree or equivalent;</li>
<li>a salary of at least AED 15,000 ($4,100) per month.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Freelance and self-employment</h3>

<ul>

<li>a bachelor's or specialist degree;</li>

To legalise, they must confirm remote employment and an income of at least $5,000 per month for Dubai or $3,500 for another emirate.</p>
<h3>Enrolment in an accredited university</h3>
<p>Foreigners can legalise for the period of their education at a local educational institution. Applicants must have sponsorship from a resident parent or an accredited university or college to which they have been admitted. A student residence permit is valid for 1 year. It can be extended for a similar period after proof of continued education.</p>
<p>A 5-year Golden Visa is available to high achievers who have completed secondary school with a grade of 95% or higher or university with a GPA of at least 3.75. They do not require sponsorship; instead, they only need a letter of recommendation from the Ministry of Education.</p>
<p>Residents are allowed to sponsor only sons under the age of 25. Male students who provide proof of attending a university for at least 1 year can continue to receive sponsorship even if they are older than this age. There are no age restrictions for unmarried daughters.</p>
<p>The list of documents includes:</p>
<ul>
<li>a certificate issued by the university or college indicating the duration of study;</li>
<li>an IELTS or TOEFL certificate confirming the level of English;</li>
<li>proof of financial solvency for living in the United Arab Emirates.</li>
</ul>
<p>After legalisation, students can work, but with restrictions on working hours.</p>
<h3>Retirement visa</h3>
<p>A retiree’s permanent residency in the UAE is issued to foreigners who have worked for at least 15 years or were at least 55 years old at the time of retirement. It is valid for 5 years. The status can be extended upon confirmation that the necessary criteria have been met.</p>
<p>Applicants must meet one of the following conditions:</p>
<ul>
<li>ownership of one or more properties worth at least AED 1 million ($272,500);</li>
<li>financial savings of at least AED 1 million ($272,500);</li>
<li>income of at least AED 15,000 ($4,100) per month for living in Dubai or AED 20,000 ($5,500) for another emirate.</li>
</ul>
<h3>Family reunification</h3>
<p>Residents can sponsor their family members if their monthly salary is at least AED 4,000 ($1,100) or AED 3,000 ($820) if they own a housing unit. Each adult foreigner applying must undergo a medical examination.</p>
<p>Key information about the family reunification programme:</p>
<ul>
<li>a mother can sponsor her children only in exceptional cases approved by the Federal Authority for Identity, Citizenship, Customs and Port Security;</li>
<li>a resident sponsor can apply for a residence permit for dependents within 60 days of their entry into the country;</li>
<li>a resident can arrange a residence permit for their parents, which will be issued on an annual basis regardless of the sponsor's status validity period;</li>
<li>the type of profession is no longer a condition for a work visa holder to determine the possibility of sponsoring their family members.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="id4">4 Steps for moving to UAE on a residence visa</h2>
<ol>
<li>Preparing documents and obtaining a short-term entry permit. To legalise property ownership through purchase, a foreigner must obtain a six-month entry permit to inspect the property and complete the formal procedures. At this stage, they must collect, translate, and legalise the required documentation.</li>
<li>Fulfilling investment conditions. Before concluding a purchase and sale transaction, the buyer must inspect the property and review all relevant documents. The investor can do this personally or authorise specialists to take on these tasks and register the property rights.</li>
<li>Undergoing a medical examination. All adult applicants must obtain a medical examination certificate at an accredited clinic. The procedure includes a chest X-ray and tests for dangerous infectious diseases.</li>
<li>Passing a background check and obtaining a residence permit. The investor submits documents and biometric data to the General Directorate of Residency and Foreigners Affairs. If the application is approved, they and their family members receive a residence permit.</li>
</ol>
<h2 id="id5">Cases when UAE residence permit may be cancelled</h2>
<ul>
<li>Prolonged absence – if a resident is absent from the country for more than 6 consecutive months (excluding Golden Visa holders).</li>
<li>Termination of employment contract – if the residence permit is issued based on work and the contract is terminated.</li>
<li>Visa regime violation – illegal stay, working without appropriate permission, or using forged documents.</li>
<li>Crimes and offences – serious criminal offences, fraud, terrorism, or drug distribution.</li>
<li>Bankruptcy or financial debts – if a resident fails to repay loans or engages in financial fraud.</li>
<li>Public order violation – participation in political protests, extremist activities, or dissemination of information contrary to local legislation.</li>
<li>Sponsor withdrawal – if the sponsor (employer, spouse, parent) withdraws support.</li>
<li>Changes in migration legislation – if the country's laws change and the resident no longer meets the new requirements.</li>
<li>Violation of moral norms – immoral behaviour, public displays of affection, or violation of Islamic norms.</li>
<li>Failure to update data – if a resident fails to update their personal data with the relevant authorities.</li>
<li>Non-compliance with visa conditions – if a foreign national received a residence permit as an investor but failed to fulfil investment obligations.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="id7">Best cities to live in UAE</h2>

English is widely spoken, the infrastructure meets world standards, and the laws are more flexible than in neighbouring emirates.</p>

<h2 id="id9">Rights and obligations of residents in the UAE</h2>
<p>Residents can reside legally, work, conduct business (with the appropriate permission), open bank accounts, rent and purchase property in designated zones, and pursue education. They can also sponsor visas for relatives.</p>
<p>Obligations include compliance with the country's laws, payment of taxes (if applicable), timely renewal of residence permits, and respect for local traditions and behavioural norms. Violation of laws can lead to deportation.</p>

[item question="How to stay living in the UAE?"]Residents wishing to stay forever can naturalise after 30 years of residence in the country's territory. For people of Arab origin, the period is reduced to 37 years. Property owners may be offered citizenship by nomination, but this is the authorities' decision, not a programme with a fixed investment amount.[/item]
[item question="Can I get a residence permit in the Emirates by buying property?"]Yes, foreign property buyers can obtain a 2-year residence permit or a 10-year Golden Visa. How the categories differ and which documents each requires is explained in a separate guide to <a href="[:ALIAS_URL:]/faq/how-to-obtain-uae-residency/">UAE residency</a>.[/item]
[item question="Can I bring pets to the UAE?"]Yes, you must obtain an international veterinary passport, microchip the animal, vaccinate it, obtain an import permit, test for rabies antibodies, treat for parasites, and obtain a veterinary certificate. The import of certain dog breeds into the country is prohibited.[/item]
