<p>There are several ways to obtain a residency in Bahrain. Popular options include the 10-year golden visa, which is available to the following categories of people:</p>
<ul>
<li>buyers of real estate worth BHD 130,000 (USD&nbsp;345,800) or more;</li>
<li>retirees with a monthly income of BHD 2,000 (USD&nbsp;5,305) or BHD 4,000 (USD&nbsp;10,610);</li>
<li>employees with a monthly income of BHD 2,000 (USD&nbsp;5,305) or more;</li>
<li>foreign nationals with outstanding talents.</li>
</ul>
<p>After 25 years of legal permanent residence in the Kingdom (15 years for those of Arab descent), residents can apply for citizenship by naturalisation. For women who have obtained a residence permit through marriage to a Bahraini citizen, this period is reduced to 5 years.</p>

<h2 id="id1">Advantages of residency in Bahrain</h2>
<ul>
<li>Long-term residency. The residence permit allows foreign nationals to reside in the Kingdom for an extended period and to enter and leave the country without difficulty. For example, a golden visa allows one to stay in the country for up to 10 years, and the validity of the document can be extended. The status provides owners with greater stability and confidence in the future and opens up additional opportunities for business, work and investment.</li>
<li>Opening a bank account. Residency dramatically simplifies opening an account in local banks, which is important for personal financial transactions and business. The state's banking system is one of the most developed in the region. The stable economy and strict regulations of the Central Bank provide a high level of protection for deposits and banking operations.</li>
<li>Access to medical and educational services. The country is known for its high level of healthcare. Both public and private medical institutions offer quality services that meet international standards. Many hospitals and clinics are equipped with modern facilities and the staff consists of qualified doctors, including specialists from Europe and the USA. Residents can choose schools and universities that participate in international programmes, including those from the United Kingdom, the United States and France.</li>
<li>Sponsorship of family members. Residence permit holders can sponsor their relatives, including spouses, children under 24 and parents. The family can live together in a stable and safe country with access to its infrastructure, medical and educational systems.</li>
<li>Tax benefits. The Kingdom does not tax personal income, capital gains, inheritance, gifts, dividends, interest or royalties. There is no separate property tax, but foreign homeowners pay an annual municipal levy of 10% of the rental value assessed by the municipality. It is a favourable location for entrepreneurs, investors and highly paid professionals who find it important to retain a large portion of their income.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Golden Visa</h3>
<p>The following categories of foreign nationals can apply for the golden visa:</p>
<ul>
<li>Pensioners whose monthly income is at least BHD 4,000 (USD&nbsp;10,610);</li>
<li>Pensioners who have worked in the state for at least 15 years, of which during the last 5 years, their pension payments amounted to at least BHD 2,000 (USD&nbsp;5,305) per month;</li>
<li>Owners of houses and <a href="[:ALIAS_URL:]/property/bahrein/apartments/">apartments in Bahrain</a> with a total value of at least BHD 130,000 (USD&nbsp;345,800) at the time of purchase (until November 2025, BHD&nbsp;200,000 was required);</li>
<li>Individuals with outstanding talents recognised by a government agency in specific fields;</li>
<li>Employees who have worked in the Kingdom for at least 5 years, during which their average salary was at least BHD 2,000 (USD&nbsp;5,305) per month.</li>
</ul>
<p>Bahrain Golden visa requirements include proof of high qualifications. For example, scientists must provide a doctoral diploma or degree in one of the priority scientific fields; entrepreneurs must provide permission from an accredited business incubator, and inventors must provide a patent. Applicants must also possess a certificate confirming their designation as gifted.</p>
<p>The golden visa costs BHD&nbsp;300 (about USD&nbsp;800) for each applicant, family members included, and the application review costs BHD&nbsp;4 (about USD&nbsp;11). Living costs, cities and schools worth considering in advance are described in an article on <a href="[:ALIAS_URL:]/faq/how-to-move-to-bahrain/">moving to Bahrain</a>.</p>
<h3>Self-sponsorship</h3>
<p>A self-sponsorship programme is available to foreign nationals, enabling them to legalise their stay in the Kingdom without requiring support from employers or family members. Buyers of real estate worth BHD 50,000 (USD&nbsp;132,600) or more, investors in businesses worth BHD 100,000 (USD&nbsp;265,200) or more and retirees can participate in the programme. Thresholds in neighbouring countries differ many times over; see more on <a href="[:ALIAS_URL:]/faq/how-much-do-you-need-to-invest-in-property-to-get-residency-in-the-gulf-states/">how much to invest in property for residency</a> in each of them.</p>
<p>The first two categories of individuals must demonstrate a monthly income of BHD 500 (approximately USD 1,326) or more. A guarantor – a trusted individual or organisation, such as an agent or representative – must submit the documents and ensure that all conditions are fulfilled. The permit can be valid for 2, 5 or 10 years. Residents are eligible to renew their status.</p>
<p>"Registering a company in Bahrain is not enough on its own to get a residence permit. It is granted to the owner or partner once the company is registered and licensed for its activity. In most sectors a foreigner can own a company in full, so a local partner is usually not needed," explains a corporate lawyer from Manama.</p>

<h3>Employment</h3>
<p>Individuals who have entered into an employment contract with a local company may be eligible to apply for a residence permit. The employer must obtain a work permit for a foreign employee by submitting an application to the Labour Market Regulatory Authority (LMRA). The following documents will be required:</p>
<ul>
<li>A license (for certain types of companies);</li>
<li>An employment contract or agreement in Arabic, on letterhead, containing the employee's details, the signatures of the parties and the company seal;</li>
<li>A certificate of medical examination for an employee who is outside the Kingdom (the examination must be conducted at an authorised medical centre or any other official medical facility);</li>
<li>A letter or certificate of non-objection issued by the relevant authority for specialised professions.</li>
</ul>
<p>If the application is approved, the employer will receive an identification number and pay the state fees. Biometric data must be surrendered at the LMRA office located at the international airport. An employee can sponsor a spouse and children under 24 with a net monthly income of BHD&nbsp;400 (about USD&nbsp;1,060) or more. The BHD 1,000 (approximately USD 2,652) threshold applies only to bringing parents or children over 24.</p>
<h3>Study at accredited universities</h3>
<p>Foreign nationals planning to study at a local university must obtain a non-immigrant visa to enter the Kingdom. Processing typically takes no more than three months. The applicant prepares a package of documents, including confirmation of enrollment in the course and receipts for tuition fee payments.</p>
<p>A student residence permit is issued in conjunction with a student visa. If the applicant has not obtained it before the start of the academic semester, they can enter the country on a visitor visa but will have to pay additional fees and state duties. If the application is approved, the foreign national receives a letter granting permission to stay and study in the state.</p>
<p>The residency period is 5 years for students studying in local universities or 1 year for students studying abroad. Holders of this permit category cannot work in the state.</p>
<h3>For family members</h3>
<p>A residence permit may be issued to family members of a resident or citizen, namely spouses, parents and children under 24 years of age. Documents such as relatives' passports and birth and marriage certificates must confirm the existence of family ties.</p>
<p>Holders of this category of permit are not permitted to work in the state. It can be issued for single and multiple entries into the Kingdom.</p>

<h2 id="id6">Possible reasons for denial of residence visas in Bahrain</h2>
<ul>
<li>Failure to meet Bahrain residence permit requirements. The Kingdom's legislation sets strict criteria for obtaining a residence permit, including income, employment, investments or family ties. If an applicant does not meet the minimum requirements, their application may be rejected.</li>
<li>Visa violation. Any violations of migration law, including exceeding the period of stay on a visa and working without authorisation, may result in the denial of a resident visa. Local services closely monitor the migration history of applicants.</li>
<li>Problems with documentation. Errors in the application, submitting an incomplete set of documents, a lack of certified translations, or forged documents may result in rejection.</li>
<li>Lack of financial stability. When applying for a residence permit, it is necessary to confirm the presence of stable income or financial assets. Insufficient income or questionable sources of funding may result in a refusal.</li>
<li>Criminal record. Applicants with criminal records or open criminal cases may face denial. Offences involving fraud and violence are taken especially seriously.</li>
<li>Health problems. Applicants for residency must undergo a medical examination. Identified serious communicable diseases or conditions that threaten public health may be grounds for rejecting the application.</li>
<li>Threat to national security. If the applicant is blacklisted, has ties to extremist organisations or poses a potential threat to the country, the residence permit will be denied.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="id7">Rights and obligations of a Bahrain resident</h2>
<p>Residents are entitled to legal residence, employment (with the appropriate visa), healthcare, education, and access to banking services. Additionally, many residence permit holders are eligible to open a business.</p>

<p>Residents' obligations include obeying the country's laws, paying taxes and fees (if their status requires it) and respecting local traditions and behavior. Employees must adhere to the terms of their employment contract, and entrepreneurs must comply with relevant commercial and financial laws. A foreigner must renew their residence permit in Bahrain on time. Violation of laws may result in fines, deportation or criminal liability.</p>
<h2 id="id8">How to extend your residence permit in Bahrain</h2>
<p>To extend a residence permit, a foreign national must apply to the Immigration Department (NPRA) at least four weeks before the expiration of the current document. At the same time, the applicant must confirm that they still meet the conditions under which the residency was initially issued. The package of documents is similar to the one submitted initially. In addition, only the expiring residence permit will be required.</p>
<p>"Renewals are most often held up by small things: an expired insurance policy or a bank statement older than six months. It is easier to gather the documents 1.5–2 months before the status runs out," advises a visa coordinator in Manama.</p>
