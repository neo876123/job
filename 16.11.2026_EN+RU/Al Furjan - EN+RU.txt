<p>Located 10 minutes’ drive from the coast of the Persian Gulf, Al Furjan is a relatively young community in Dubai. It offers all conditions for a comfortable lifestyle for families with children. The construction of the community began in 2008, and the main infrastructure and residential projects were handed over in 2022. Building has not stopped, though: in 2021–2022 Nakheel launched the Murooj Al Furjan and Tilal Al Furjan villa enclaves, and in 2025–2026 other developers announced new residential projects here. Most of the new townhouses, villas and apartments are luxury <a href="../property/dubai/">property in Dubai</a>; however, their prices are lower than those of similar properties in the city center.</p>
<p>The new community features 30 parks with walking paths and outdoor leisure infrastructure. The main developer of Al Furjan is a large real estate development company, Nakheel, known for Dubai's iconic artificial island Palm Jumeirah and many other major residential and commercial projects. Nakheel now sits within Dubai Holding Real Estate alongside Meraas and Dubai Properties. Some plots in the community were developed by other companies, including Azizi, Danube, Deyaar, NABNI and Symbolic.</p>
<p>Al Furjan covers 560 hectares. According to Nakheel and the Dubai Media Office, as of May 2021 it had about 26,000 residents and a housing stock of 1,700 villas and townhouses plus 2,800 apartments. More homes have been added since, but no updated total for 2026 has been published.</p>

<h2 id="id1">Location</h2>
<p>Al Furjan is part of the larger community of Jebel Ali District. The area is located between two highways: Sheikh Zayed Road, which runs along the entire seacoast, and Sheikh Mohammed Bin Zayed Road, which will take you to the city center.</p>
<p>The new community is bordered by the elite residential communities Discovery Gardens and The Gardens, which have many parks, landscaped gardens, sports and leisure areas. Another neighbour is Jebel Ali Village, where Nakheel began handing over 892 rebuilt villas and townhouses to owners in August 2026.</p>
<p>Dubai Marina is 10 km from Al Furjan. It is popular for its waterfront promenade lined with trendy restaurants and shops. The street runs along the bay with marinas. Pedestrians can enjoy beautiful views of the sea and snow-white yachts. It also has a free sandy beach with water activities, a large shopping mall, a parachuting centre, a number of luxury hotels and many other facilities.</p>
<p>Another popular spot within a 10-minute drive from Al Furjan is the large Oriental-style Ibn Battuta Mall. The territory of the mall is divided into several zones, each dedicated to a different country. The mall houses shops with clothes, accessories, electronics, children's toys, jewellery and many other goods. Here you can find restaurants of different cuisines, entertainment centres for children, a supermarket and a cinema.</p>

<h2 id="id5">Selecting property and features of buying off-plan</h2>
<p>Al Furjan has a wide range of residential properties. Here you can find relatively inexpensive options for studio apartments. There is no published average price for studios in the community, while hotel apartments sold for AED 655,505 ($178,500) on average, based on DLD deals over the 12 months to November 2026. If you are planning to buy an <a href="../property/dubai/new-apartments/off-plan/">apartment in Dubai</a> for a residence visa, check the 2026 rules. Industry outlets say that on 29 April 2026 Dubai dropped the property price floor for single owners seeking the investor visa valid for two years; with joint purchases, each co-owner’s share starts at AED 400,000 ($108,900). For the ten-year golden visa, DLD sets the property threshold at AED 2 million ($544,600). A one-bedroom apartment in Al Furjan sold for AED 1,131,895 ($308,200) on average in the same deals. The price of villas and townhouses averages AED 5,692,114 ($1.55 million).</p>
<p>If you are ready to wait for an apartment or a house, then you can look for an off-plan unit. Today, developers offer interest-free instalments. You pay according to the plan given by the developer while the property is being built. In this case, the final payment is made at the time of handing over the keys or within a few years after the project is completed. In 2026 the most common schedules are 70/30 and 80/20: before handover the buyer pays 70–80% of the price. A price rise by handover is not guaranteed — the table above shows how prices in the community itself have moved over the past year.</p>

<p>How a ready-property purchase works:</p>
<ol>
<li>The parties sign Form F, the unified memorandum of the deal (MoU); a 10% deposit is market practice.</li>
<li>The developer consents to the sale: an electronic e-NOC comes through Dubai REST.</li>
<li>The transfer is completed via Dubai Now or in person at a registration trustee; non-residents do it with just a passport.</li>
<li>DLD issues the title deed in the buyer’s name.</li>
</ol>
<p>An off-plan unit is bought under a sale and purchase agreement (SPA) with the developer, after which the agreement goes into the Oqood register.</p>
<p>On top of one-off payments, the owner pays an annual service charge. There is no official rate for the community as a whole: it is approved for each building. According to agency estimates, apartments in Al Furjan typically fall within AED 12–17 per sq ft, which is AED 129–183 per m² a year; villas pay AED 5–6 per sq ft (AED 54–65 per m² after conversion). The exact figure for each building appears in the Service Charge Index run by DLD.</p>
