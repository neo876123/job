<p>Buying property gives the right to a residence permit in all four countries, but the entry threshold varies several times over. In Qatar it is QAR&nbsp;730,000 (about $200,000), in Bahrain BHD&nbsp;130,000 (about $345,000), and in Saudi Arabia SAR&nbsp;4&nbsp;million (about $1.07m). In Oman, an owner's residence permit has no minimum property value, while the Golden Residency requires OMR&nbsp;200,000 (about $520,000). In every country, the residence permit rests on ownership of the property and depends on its value at the time of purchase. This article compares the conditions, terms and restrictions, and explains what happens to the residence permit when the property is sold.</p>
<p>The rules for buying the property are set out in the article&nbsp;on <a href="[:ALIAS_URL:]/faq/can-a-foreigner-buy-property-in-saudi-arabia-qatar-oman-and-bahrain">whether a foreigner can buy property in the Gulf</a>, and taxes and fees in the article&nbsp;on <a href="[:ALIAS_URL:]/faq/what-taxes-and-fees-does-a-foreigner-pay-when-buying-and-owning-property-in-the-gulf-states">what taxes and fees does a foreigner pay when buying and owning property in the Gulf States</a>. The general procedure for obtaining a residence permit in each country is covered separately: <a href="[:ALIAS_URL:]/faq/how-to-get-a-residence-permit-in-saudi-arabia/">a Saudi residence permit</a>, <a href="[:ALIAS_URL:]/faq/how-to-get-a-residence-permit-in-qatar/">a Qatar residence permit</a>, <a href="[:ALIAS_URL:]/faq/obtaining-a-residence-permit-in-oman/">an Oman residence permit</a> and <a href="[:ALIAS_URL:]/faq/how-to-get-a-residence-permit-in-bahrain/">a Bahrain residence permit</a>.</p>

<h2 id="id2">Saudi Arabia</h2>
<p>The residence permit for property owners is part of the Premium Residency programme. The "real estate owner" category requires a certified value of at least SAR&nbsp;4&nbsp;million in residential property in designated areas. The status is tied to ownership of the property or to certain rights over it.</p>
<p>Consultants name two further conditions that are worth checking on the programme's official portal before buying:</p>
<ul>
<li>the property is completed and not mortgaged;</li>
<li>the value is confirmed by an accredited valuer.</li>
</ul>
<p>Buying a property for this amount does not in itself give a residence permit: the application is reviewed by the Premium Residency Centre. The status lasts while the buyer keeps the property. Approval costs SAR&nbsp;4,000 (about $1,070) in state duty, and reviewing the application adds $170. Open sources do not say whether the threshold is counted for a single property or for several together. This should be established with the centre before the contract is signed.</p>
<p>The law on property ownership by foreigners, which came into force on 22 January 2026, widened the choice of properties. The zones where a foreigner may buy are described in the article on <a href="[:ALIAS_URL:]/faq/can-a-foreigner-buy-property-in-saudi-arabia-qatar-oman-and-bahrain">can a foreigner buy property in Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Oman and Bahrain</a>.</p>

<p>According to the Ministry of Justice, a residence permit is issued without a sponsor to the buyer of a property worth QAR&nbsp;730,000 or more. The condition is a stay in the country of at least 90 days a year. Permanent residency itself is not part of this tier: the status is given after two decades in the country or for special merit, with approvals capped at 100 a year.</p>
<p>The property must be in a zone where foreigners are allowed to own property. The list of such zones grew to ten in June 2026. These zones are described in the article&nbsp;on <a href="[:ALIAS_URL:]/faq/can-a-foreigner-buy-property-in-saudi-arabia-qatar-oman-and-bahrain">can a foreigner buy property in Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Oman and Bahrain</a>.</p>
<h2 id="id4">Oman</h2>
<p>Oman has two routes, and they differ by threshold.</p>
<p><strong>Property owner residence permit (Owner Visa).</strong> It was introduced by Royal Oman Police Decision No. 87/2026, which came into force on 22 June 2026. The residence permit is issued without a sponsor to the owner of a property on the basis of a certificate from the competent authority. Open sources give no minimum property value.</p>
<p>Conditions according to consultants and the local press:</p>
<ul>
<li>the term is from 6 months to one year, with the option to renew;</li>
<li>each entry gives the right to stay for up to three months;</li>
<li>the residence permit extends to the spouse and first-degree relatives;</li>
<li>if ownership passes to another person, the owner's residence permit and those of family members end.</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Golden Residency.</strong> The programme was launched on 1 September 2025. According to Invest Oman, the minimum amount is OMR&nbsp;200,000 (about $520,000), the property must be completed and located in an integrated tourism complex (ITC), and it needs a valid title deed. The term is 10 years, renewable, and the family is included. Some reviews give different amounts and terms, so the conditions should be confirmed with Invest Oman before paying.</p>

<h2 id="id5">Bahrain</h2>
<p>The Golden Residency for property owners in Bahrain is available for property worth BHD&nbsp;130,000 or more (about $345,000). The Nationality, Passports and Residence Affairs authority (NPRA) cut the threshold from BHD&nbsp;200,000 in late 2025.</p>
<p>How the threshold is counted, according to the programme's official page:</p>
<ul>
<li>the value at the time of purchase is taken into account;</li>
<li>the threshold can be met with one property or several, provided the combined value of the applicant's shares reaches BHD&nbsp;130,000.</li>
</ul>
<p>Additional conditions cited by consultants: the property is in a freehold zone, and ownership is confirmed by a document from the Survey and Land Registration Bureau (SLRB). The zones are described in the article on <a href="[:ALIAS_URL:]/faq/can-a-foreigner-buy-property-in-saudi-arabia-qatar-oman-and-bahrain">can a foreigner buy property in Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Oman and Bahrain</a>.</p>
<p>State fees, according to the official service page, total BHD&nbsp;304 (about $809): BHD&nbsp;4 for submitting the application and BHD&nbsp;300 for issuing the permit. A medical examination, insurance and a resident card are paid for separately. The programme sets no minimum period of stay in the country.</p>
<p>Sources differ on the term: some describe the residence permit as 10-year, others as indefinite with a fee every 10 years. Which version applies should be confirmed with the NPRA.</p>

<p>If you plan to sell the property, find out in advance how to keep the right to reside: buy another qualifying property, or switch to another type of residence permit, for example through work.</p>
<h2 id="id7">How to prepare your application</h2>
<p>The general procedure for all countries:</p>
<ol>
<li>Choose the country and programme by threshold and term.</li>
<li>Check that the property is in a zone where foreigners are allowed to own, and that ownership will be registered rather than a lease.</li>
<li>Find out how the value is counted: by the contract, by the title deed or by a valuation.</li>
<li>Confirm the conditions with an official source: the Premium Residency Centre; Aqarat and the Ministry of Justice in Qatar; Invest Oman and the Royal Oman Police; the NPRA.</li>
<li>Gather the documents: passport, title deed, family members' documents, photographs. The exact list depends on the country and programme.</li>
<li>Budget in advance for fees, the medical examination and insurance.</li>
</ol>
<p>"The threshold is counted by the value at the time of purchase, and what matters is the price stated in the title deed. If you are a few thousand short of the threshold, it is better to reconsider the property before the deal: once it is registered, correcting the value will be harder," says an immigration consultant from Manama.</p>
<p>"Clients often compare only the thresholds, but what the status gives matters more. In Qatar, QAR&nbsp;730,000 comes with a requirement to spend 90 days a year in the country, while Bahrain has no such rule, even though its threshold is higher," notes an overseas property consultant from Doha.</p>

<h2 id="id8">Who the programmes are not for</h2>
<ul>
<li>Those who are not ready to invest from $200,000 in Qatar or $345,000 in Bahrain. In the Gulf states, a residence permit can also be obtained on other grounds: work, business, retirement.</li>
<li>Those who buy with bank money. For Saudi Arabia, consultants name the absence of a mortgage on the property as a condition.</li>
<li>Those who will not be able to live in Qatar for at least 90 days a year. This is a condition of the owner's residence permit.</li>
<li>Those who need the right to work. In Oman, according to consultants, the owner's residence permit does not replace a work permit.</li>
<li>Those who count on citizenship. The residence permits in the programmes covered here do not give citizenship.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="id9">What has changed in the conditions since 2025</h2>
<p>Status of the rules: 9 November 2026.</p>
<ul>
<li>On 1 September 2025, Oman launched the Golden Residency. One route is completed property in an ITC worth OMR&nbsp;200,000 or more.</li>
<li>In late 2025, Bahrain cut the Golden Residency threshold for property owners from BHD&nbsp;200,000 to BHD&nbsp;130,000, a reduction of 35%.</li>
<li>On 22 January 2026, Saudi Arabia's law on property ownership by foreigners came into force. Foreigners gained the right to buy property in designated zones.</li>
<li>On 22 June 2026, Decision No. 87/2026 came into force in Oman. It introduced a sponsor-free residence permit for property owners.</li>
<li>In June 2026, Qatar added the Simaisma resort to the areas open to foreign ownership, bringing the total to ten.</li>
</ul>
